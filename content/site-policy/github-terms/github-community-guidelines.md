Millions of developers around the world host projects on GitHub. These guidelines describe what a safe, welcoming, and productive GitHub community looks like and how we expect people to interact on the platform.

Scope
- These guidelines are guidance for contributors, maintainers, and organizations using GitHub.com. They do not replace GitHub's Terms of Service.

## Who this applies to
- Contributors, maintainers, and organizations using GitHub.
- Public and private repositories hosted on GitHub.com.
- Community spaces like issues, pull requests, discussions, and profile pages.

## Core principles
- Be welcoming and open-minded — welcome newcomers and value diverse perspectives.
- Be respectful — critique ideas, not people; avoid abusive or demeaning language.
- Be empathetic — recognize that people come from different backgrounds and experiences.
- Be constructive — give actionable feedback and write clearly and kindly.

## Concrete examples (do / don't)
- Do: Ask clarifying questions in a code review and provide suggested fixes or references.
- Don't: Post personal attacks, insults, or slurs directed at a person or group.
- Do: Create a CONTRIBUTING or CODE_OF_CONDUCT file (for example, `/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`) to set expectations for your repo and link to it from the README.
- Don't: Share private information (doxxing) or threats of violence.
- Do: Use issue templates to request additional information from reporters and to triage bug reports and security concerns.
- Don't: Repeatedly harass or target a user across multiple issues, PRs, or accounts.
- Do: Use respectful, technical language when explaining why a change is needed; include examples and steps to reproduce when reporting bugs.
- Don't: Use sustained trolling behavior, coordinated harassment, or attempts to silence legitimate contributors.

## More real-world scenarios
- Hate speech & slurs: Any direct use of slurs, epithets, or language intended to degrade a protected class is not acceptable. Remove or report content that includes these terms used to harass.
- Doxxing: Posting private or intimate information about someone (home address, phone numbers, non-public social profiles, government ID details) to intimidate or shame is strictly prohibited.
- Threats & violence: Threats of physical harm, sexual violence, or incitement to violence are disallowed and should be reported immediately.
- Impersonation & fraud: Creating accounts that impersonate another user, organization, or project to mislead or defraud is not acceptable.
- Spam & malicious links: Repeated, irrelevant, or promotional posts and posts that include malware or phishing links should be removed and reported.
- Targeted harassment campaigns: Coordinated attempts to flood or harass an individual across multiple repos or accounts are prohibited.
- Abuse of project tools: Using issues, PRs, or comments to continually harass maintainers (for example, by reopening closed discussions, creating duplicate abusive issues) is unacceptable.

## Maintainers: tools and best practices
- Communicate expectations — add `CONTRIBUTING` and `CODE_OF_CONDUCT` files and link to them from your README.
- Moderate comments — users with write access can edit, hide, or remove comments that violate repository rules.
- Lock conversations — when threads get off-topic, hostile, or repetitive, lock them to stop further comments.
- Block users — block specific users from interacting with your personal account if needed.
- Limit interactions — use temporary interaction limits for repos experiencing spam, harassment, or targeted abuse.
- Use templates — issue and PR templates help gather structured reports and make moderation faster.
- Create moderation workflows — document how maintainers triage reports, escalate sensitive cases, and communicate actions back to the community.
- Security: publish a `SECURITY.md` and clearly document how to report security vulnerabilities.

## How to report content or behavior
- Use the built-in reporting tools on the offending content (issue, comment, or profile).
- If you need additional support, contact GitHub Support: {% raw %}{% data variables.contact.contact_support %}{% endraw %} (or replace with https://support.github.com/contact if you prefer an explicit URL).
- When reporting, include helpful context: direct links to the content, a brief description of why it violates guidelines, and any relevant timestamps or thread links.
- For legal emergencies or imminent threats to safety, contact local law enforcement in addition to reporting on GitHub.

## Enforcement and transparency
- We rely on community reports and automated detection; each report is reviewed case-by-case by our moderation team.
- Possible outcomes include removing offending content, limiting visibility, hiding user accounts or organizations from public view, or suspending accounts.
- We apply a human-centered, proportional approach when responding to reports and aim to provide options for remediation when appropriate.
- Users may not always receive public details about moderation decisions due to privacy and safety considerations, but we strive for consistent, fair enforcement.
